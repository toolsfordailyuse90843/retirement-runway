How is this calculated?

On track for is what your portfolio grows to by your retirement age: your current balance plus your monthly contributions, compounded monthly at the expected return.

You actually need is the portfolio that can pay for the part of your lifestyle that other income does not cover. That amount is raised by inflation up to the year you retire, then divided by the withdrawal rate you can safely take each year.

OMERS contributions use the 2026 Primary Plan rates: 9.0% of pay up to $74,600 and 14.6% above it for a normal retirement age of 65, or 9.2% and 15.8% for age 60. Your employer pays the same amount. The pension is 2% of your best five years of pay for each year of service. At 65 it drops by 0.675% of pay (up to about $69,200) for each year of service, because the bridge benefit ends. OMERS lists rate changes from January 2027, so check omers.com. More in the OMERS member handbook.

HOOPP contributions are 6.9% of pay up to $74,600 and 9.2% above it, and the employer adds $1.26 for every $1 paid. HOOPP says rates stay unchanged through 2027. The pension is 1.5% of pay up to about $69,200 plus 2% of pay above that, for each year of service. Retiring before 65 also earns a bridge benefit of 0.5% of pay up to that amount for each year of service, until 65. Details are on the HOOPP pension page and in the HOOPP handbook. HOOPP has raised the rate on some past service years, so an official estimate may be higher.

Two people. When you add a partner, you both retire at the same time, so your partner's age moves with yours. Each pension is worked out on its own pay and service, and the two are added together against one household lifestyle. If one pension cannot start yet (it starts at 55, or 50 for OMERS with age 60), your portfolio covers that income until it does.

All plans assume you stay with a plan employer until you retire, that pay keeps up with inflation, and that pensions keep up with inflation too. Neither plan guarantees that: HOOPP's inflation increases are not guaranteed for service after 2005, and OMERS pension earned from 2023 onward is not guaranteed to be fully indexed.

Retiring early uses a 5% cut for each year before the normal retirement age: 65 or 60 for OMERS, 60 for HOOPP, where 30 years of service also avoids the cut. HOOPP's own examples show cuts of about 3% to 6% a year, so use its Pension Estimator for the exact amount. OMERS also applies other rules to some members, such as the 90 Factor.

Estimates in Canadian dollars. Returns are not guaranteed, and the figures ignore taxes, fees, CPP, OAS and changes in your spending. This is a planning aid, not financial advice.
